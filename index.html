I am Ben

Composing Digital Media Portfolio

Introduction to New Media


Code and Technology Narrative

In this project, I created a narrative about my experiences with technology and coding. I used digital images and wrote to explain how technology has influenced my life. This project helped me think about how words and visual design can work together to communicate a personal story.

Tessa's Journey

For this project, I used digital media to present Tessa's journey in a clear and visually interesting way. I considered the order of the information, the images, and the overall layout. I revised the project to make the story easier for the audience to follow.

The Perfect Post

In this project, I examined how design choices affect the way a social media post communicates with an audience. I considered the image, wording, organization, and visual appeal of the post. This helped me understand how digital content can be designed for a specific purpose and audience.

Course Goals


1. Consider technology’s role in acts of making

In my Code and Technology Narrative, I wrote about building platforms and using tools like GitHub, Supabase, and Netlify. Making the narrative into a web page let me show images and my Dear Data visualization alongside my writing. In Tessa’s Journey, the links between pages let the reader move through the story instead of reading everything on one page. These projects helped me see how technology affects the way I tell a story.

2. Embrace experimentation

In The Perfect Post, I showed bright, blurry, dark, and poorly cropped versions of the same moment. These different versions show how editing a picture can change the way someone sees it. I also put the polished post before the details it leaves out, so readers see the picture first and then learn what it didn't show as they scroll.

3.Engage with others in a workshop environment

During the workshop, I shared my work with classmates and received feedback about what they liked. Some classmates also helped me figure out how to fix images that were not showing up on my pages. Their help made me better able to present my projects because viewers could see the images alongside my writing. Working with others helped me solve problems that I was having trouble figuring out on my own.

4. Practice revision within and across media forms

When revising this portfolio, I added links to the project pictures so viewers could open the full projects. I also made my course goal explanations more specific. For example, instead of just saying I experimented with images, I explained how The Perfect Post uses four versions of the same moment. These changes made the portfolio easier to use and helped explain the purpose behind my work.

5. Learn to present your work

In this portfolio, I used a picture, a title, and a description to introduce each project. For Tessa’s Journey, the picture gives viewers a preview of the story before they open it. For The Perfect Post, the description explains how the project questions what people see on social media. Adding links to the pictures also lets viewers explore the full work instead of only seeing a screenshot.

Sound and Image


Oakland Sound Composition

For this project, I recorded outdoor sounds in Schenley Park, including wind and water, and edited them together in Audacity. My goal was to capture a sense of the park through sound. This project made me pay closer attention to background sounds that I might normally overlook and consider how recordings can communicate the atmosphere of a place.

Exploratory Podcast: Attendance in College

In this podcast, I explored whether attendance should count toward a college student's grade. I started with my own opinion and considered research about attendance and academic success. I used Audacity to edit the recording. The project challenged me to consider why professors value attendance while also questioning whether attendance points fairly measure what students learn.


Photo/Text Collage: Small Details, Strong Feelings

This collage responds to Roland Barthes's Camera Lucida by pairing black and white photographs with a quotation about studium. I used the title Small Details, Strong Feelings to connect ordinary scenes with the personal responses photographs can create. The placement of the photographs, large headings, and open space encourages viewers to slow down and look closely.

Photo Essay: A Neighborhood After Dark

For my photo essay, I took a nighttime walk and photographed streetlights, a bus stop, storefronts, and houses. I arranged the photos in Premiere Pro and added short captions and music to create a calm nighttime atmosphere. The project explores the connection between quiet sidewalks and the lights showing that everyday life is still going on.

Course Goals


1. Consider technology's role in acts of making

Using Audacity for both my sound composition and podcast showed me how the same tool can support different kinds of projects. In the sound composition, I worked with wind and water recordings to communicate the atmosphere of Schenley Park. In the podcast, I worked with my voice and a written script to explore a question. Premiere Pro let me combine photos, captions, and music in my photo essay. These projects helped me understand how the tools I use affect the way an audience experiences my ideas.


2. Embrace experimentation

The photo essay gave me a chance to begin with observation instead of a fully planned story. I walked through the neighborhood at night and took pictures of things that caught my attention. After looking through the pictures, I noticed a connection between the quiet streets and the lights in houses and storefronts. This helped me see how a project's direction can develop through the process of collecting material. Recording sounds in Schenley Park also encouraged me to pay attention to my surroundings in a different way.


3. Engage with others in a workshop environment

During this unit, one of my classmates helped me download Adobe so I could use Premiere Pro. My classmates also gave me feedback on my podcast, pointing out that I could improve the flow by sounding more conversational instead of sounding like I was reading from a script. This helped me understand how delivery affects a listener's experience. They also responded positively to my collage, which gave me more confidence in my work. These conversations showed me how classmates can help with both learning new tools and recognizing ways to improve a project.


4. Practice revision within and across media forms

Turning my 5 minute write question into a podcast required me to think about how a written explanation would work when spoken. Listeners need to follow the question, research, and my responses without being able to look back at a paragraph. My photo essay involved a different kind of revision, arranging separate photographs into a sequence and adding captions to connect them. These projects helped me think about revision as a process of shaping material for a particular part. If I revise my photo essay further, I would like to try smoother transitions, more varied camera angles, and sounds recorded during the walk.


5. Learn to present your work

Each project required a different way of presenting an idea. The sound composition relies on recordings to suggest a place, while the podcast uses spoken explanation to guide listeners through a question. The collage places photos and quotes together on a page, and the photo essay presents images over time with captions and music. Bringing these projects into my portfolio gives me a chance to explain their purposes and provide links so viewers can experience the full work.
